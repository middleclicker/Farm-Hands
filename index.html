Winter Wheat

What winter wheat is for

Winter wheat grain is milled into flour for bread, biscuits, and other foods. It may also be used as animal feed. Its straw can be baled for bedding after harvest.

Why we grow it

The grain and straw give the farm two useful products. The real harvest depends on weather, soil, and care; this first field is our chance to learn.

Charlie's winter wheat field guide

Uses. Grain can be milled into flour for bread and biscuits or used as animal feed. Straw left after harvest can be baled for bedding. In Willow Creek, grain and straw can be sold at the farm shop.

Planting. Drill winter wheat into a firm seedbed in September or early October. It sprouts and establishes roots before winter, slows in the cold, then resumes growth in spring. Prepare the field first: test the soil, choose a plan, turn under the grass, and break up the exposed soil with a walk-behind tiller.

Year at a glance. July–August: prepare. September–October: drill. October–November: establish. December–January: slow growth. February–March: tiller and apply spring fertiliser. April: stems extend. May–June: flowers and grain form. June–July: grain fills and ripens; harvest in July–August when ready. Wet ground can delay grading and cultivation.
